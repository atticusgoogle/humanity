How it works

  1. Ask or share

    Type a question, or something you’ve noticed: fewer fireflies, an earlier spring, a strange sound at the beach.

  2. It’s filed in the index

    Your words are matched to everyday topics, like the index at the back of a book, and your entry is added there.

  3. See the work underway

    You see real Google projects on that topic, in plain words, and ways to dig in with Gemini.

Big problems need big-time help.

Big labs, big countries, big institutions and big research departments, sure.

But they’re only a fraction of the help we need.

Science is humanity’s largest group project.

It’s built on everything the world knows, at a scale that affects us all. Yet the day-to-day work of science is still gated in ivory towers.

So we put what the world knows into Gemini, so more of the world can get involved.

Now everyone has the same Gemini tools the biggest labs use, to track down ideas or offer their own observations.

Sometimes the answers come from the places you least expect.

Science papers name a few people, then fold everyone else into two words: et al., “and others.” Here are some of the others.
